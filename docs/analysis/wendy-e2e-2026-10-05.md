## Defects

### Kimaki fork (`kimaki-tailored`)

1. **`send --model` on a locked session is silently ignored.** Evidence: lock check 4b ran on fable with no warning in the output. Callers (Wendy's `switch_thread_model`) can't tell the switch didn't apply. Suggest a warning on stderr, or a non-zero exit with `--json` `{lockedModel}`.
2. **`session search --all` is slow:** 91 s for 14 days and 162 s for all time (CLI direct, 151 and 977 sessions scanned). Wendy has to background it at 25 s, which makes every content search a two-turn affair.
3. **Status contract drift with Wendy's parser** (`kimaki-whisper/src/kimaki/api.ts:96-108`). The fork emits `updated`, `pendingQuestion`, and `lastError` without `at`. Wendy reads `lastActivityAt`, `awaitingReply` and `lastError.at`, so those come back null. That's harmless on the printer (DB fill-in) but loses data on DB-less nodes. Also latent: the parser prefers `j.status ?? j.state`. `session list --json` items carry both (`status:"idle"`, `state:"errored"`), so if the status payload ever gains `status`, errored or blocked would read as idle. Pick one name (`state`) or alias both.
4. Minor: `session status`/`read` on an unknown id exit 64 with a human error on stderr and nothing on stdout. A `--json` error envelope would let callers tell "not found" from "CLI broke".

No fork regression was observed. status, read --json --last, model get/set/lock/unlock, recover --dry-run, the stdout flush (a 35.5 MB piped `session read` ended intact), logger-to-stderr under `--json`, and the command merge all behaved as specified.

### Wendy (`kimaki-whisper`)

1. **Claimed an action she never made** (5: "I've just put a guard on it", 22:26:20). There was no `guard_thread` call and `guards.json` was unchanged. The send-claim check (`wendy.ts:858`) covers sends only, not guard, pin, recover or schedule claims. On the follow-up she stayed evasive.
2. **Fabricated session ids reach tools.** `thread_health(ses_2c5b2b253ffe94Z5z92Zb3971J)` and `recover_thread(ses_6464d524cffe7z80X0882v5Q5V, dry_run)` both ran, and neither id exists anywhere. `read_session` raised `unverified_read` but proceeded. The unverified-target guard does not cover `thread_health`/`recover_thread`. A non-dry-run recover on a hallucinated-but-real id would be destructive.
3. **`thread_health` reports IDLE for a nonexistent session** ("Last activity never. Model ?."). When `kimaki session status` exits non-zero, `sessionStatus.ts` falls back to `dbHealth`, which returns a default idle instead of NOT FOUND.
4. **Background results are invisible off-voice.** With no VC connection, `announce()` pushes to `pending` (`wendy.ts:1478`), and the text-inbox turn path never drains it. She has no tool to read pending updates. Result: 4 finished searches went unseen, she said "neither has landed" (7c), and she re-ran all-time searches. The text inbox isn't "the exact owner-turn path" in this respect.
5. **Promises not tracked.** Only 2 of 5 spoken promises produced `commitment_recorded` (missed: dark-mode "ping you the second it's done", footer "let you know when it lands", Fresnel "name the thread"). Combined with the 3-min `textPingOwner` throttle (`wendy.ts:1443`), the dark-mode completion was never pushed to the owner.
6. **Thread index ignores `state`/`lastError`** from `session list --all --json`. `thread-index.json` stores `status:"idle"` for the errored `ses_ff8c88146…`. `lookup_thread` only flags content-filter blocks (per-hit DB `filterState`), and `index_pulse` reports recency ("ACTIVE NOW (last hour)") rather than state. That's why 5c and 8 missed errored/blocked threads, and why 8 called idle threads "active".
7. **Model pins bypass Kimaki's lock.** `senses/modelPins.ts:25` writes `session_models` with `sqlite3`, so a Kimaki-locked thread was switched anyway (4c). Wendy hasn't migrated to `kimaki session model --set/--lock`. The `switch_thread_model` injected prompt still says "to balance compute load" (`wendy.ts:410`) even when the owner asked for the switch.
8. `fetch_reply` and the lookup `LIVE NOW` tail skip a 2-char latest reply ("OK") and show an older message or the latest user prompt. That's minor here but could misreport terse agents.
9. Pre-test smoke (21:35:04): the first text-inbox turn tried `telegram_reply`, which errored with "no active conversation". The inbox origin isn't distinguished in the prompt.

## Rails gaps (what the brain couldn't do, or did awkwardly)

- No **list-guards** tool, so she couldn't verify her own guard claim.
- No **org-wide blocked/errored listing**, even though `session list --all --json` has `state`/`lastError` per session. Overview questions get recency, not health.
- No **pull for pending updates or search results**. Background results are push-only and voice-only.
- **Same-title disambiguation:** three "BaseStonk Base launchpad fork" sessions, no state shown per hit, and she silently picked one.
- `recover_thread` and `switch_thread_model`/pins still use Wendy's own mechanisms (OpenCode HTTP revert, `sqlite3` writes) instead of the fork's `session recover` and `session model --lock`.
- `ask_thread` still blocks 12 s and degrades to fire-and-forget. `send --json` plus `wait --after` would remove that.
- Slow first hops (21 s, 29 s, 35 s reasoning) on update/overview turns dominate the multi-hop latency, as in lens1.

## Overall verdict: **PASS-with-issues**

The fork delivers what it promised. Authoritative blocked/errored status, fresh structured reads, a sticky and lockable model, a clean recover dry-run, the stdout flush and the slash-command merge all verified, and Wendy's reads, search plumbing, index refresh (101 s → 11 s) and blocked-state detection improved measurably. Owner-visible trust problems remain on the Wendy side: a claimed-but-not-done guard, invented session ids reaching tools, `thread_health` saying IDLE for a missing session, background results lost when the owner isn't in voice, untracked promises, and an index that can't see errored threads.
